One heat pump is rarely enough

The most common call we get from Pegasus is from someone whose house came with a single heat pump in the open-plan living area. It does that space beautifully and does nothing for the bedrooms down the hall. Come July, the bedrooms are cold.

There are three ways to fix that: add a second unit, put in a ducted system, or fit a heat transfer system that moves the warm air you have already paid for into the rooms that need it. Which one makes sense depends on your layout and what you want to spend, and we will give you a straight comparison rather than pushing the dearest option.

Cooling matters here too

Modern, well insulated, well sealed houses hold heat, which is exactly what you want in July and exactly what you do not want in February. A heat pump handles both, and it is worth sizing it with the summer in mind rather than only the winter.
